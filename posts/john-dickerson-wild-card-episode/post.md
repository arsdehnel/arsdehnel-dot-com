On a drive yesterday I was listening to [the John Dickerson episode](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/17/nx-s1-5971459/john-dickerson-slate-political-gabfest-navel-gazing) of the [Wild Card with Rachel Martin](https://www.npr.org/podcasts/510379/wild-card-with-rachel-martin) podcast.  At roughly 14:56 into the episode (according to my podcast app) Rachel asks John:

> What do we as a society gain from having individual curators of our news and information.  And what do lose in the dissolution of trust in the mainstream institutions of media?"

And his answer (and their follow-up discussion) is fascinating! I suggest you go listen to the whole section or whole episode.  But a few keys comments:

> I think it offers us a chance to regain the trust.  If you can develop a personal relationship with the audience... which is to reconnect the bonds that use to just be implicit.  Which is to say I have lived a life that has some connection with yours... I am struggling to figure out what this information is _on your behalf_. Because I respect you and your attention...it is not something that I freak you out so you pay attention... That's that way I'm going to do my job and if I'm messing it up... come let me know!

---

I think I've viewed this move to Substack and podcasts as a regression or a way for journalists to sort of ride the success of their careers into retirement.  But listening to John and Rachel talk about their careers and the opportunity these smaller scale, less flashy, and far more authentic platforms are provided is inspiring.  Listen to the podcast and think about following some of your favorite people on Substack.  This post is not a paid endorsement of anyone or anything; I just think there are good things out there but you might have to look a little harder than before.
